2025 to 2026

LightSource, from the brand down

A three-year-old procurement platform, nineteen engineers and no designer. I came in first. What follows is what I built: a brand, a design system, the core workflow, and a team that works the same way.

01 · Brand

Fifteen oranges to one

The logo was a PNG people emailed each other. "Orange" had no hex. Before the product could get a system, the company needed an identity to build it from, so the rebrand came first: one color, Wasa and Inter, a 27-page brand book, a new site and every piece sales puts in front of a customer.

#FF4B00 The most orange a screen can be.
02 · System

Learn one page, learn them all

Every feature had been built on its own, so nothing learned on one screen helped on the next. I built a small, engineering-first system in the Tailwind the team already wrote, one header, toolbar and table for every list page, and one sidebar where there had been two navs.

03 · Workflow

The workflow, not just the screens

The platform requires way too many clicks.

Senior buyer, aerospace manufacturer

A sourcing project is where buyers spend their day, and it all lived on one overloaded page. We took it apart on whiteboards for months with the engineers who would build it, then rebuilt it as a workspace: Overview as mission control, items and suppliers as first-class tabs, and nothing that throws you out of the project.

04 · Team

One designer, then a practice

It started with me. Now two more designers and about twenty engineers work from the same small set of patterns, and critique goes to the hard problems because the system answers the easy ones. Most of this was drawn on a wall with the people who built it.

I get really good feedback on the table views. This table is just more useful.
